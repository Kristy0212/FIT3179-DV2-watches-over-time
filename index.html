1. Watch History

How did watch brands emerge and evolve throughout their history?

Historical Highlight

Watch brand founding peaked between 1850–1899, with 54 brands in the dataset. This declined to 41 brands in 1900–1949 and 12 brands in 1950–1999.

Manufacturer Lifecycles

Some long-established watch manufacturers experienced revivals after their original operations ended, showing how historic watch brands can return years after becoming defunct. The founding years mark the beginning of each manufacturer's timeline.

2. Global Watchmaking

Where are watch brands located, and how did watchmaking spread?

Leading Watchmaking Origins

Switzerland dominates the dataset with 123 brands (27.39%), more than twice the United States' 60 brands (13.36%). Japan ranks third among the highlighted countries, with 31 brands (6.90%).

European Dominance

Switzerland's leading share highlights Europe's historical importance in watchmaking. Although the United States and Japan also have notable representation, Switzerland remains the strongest individual country of origin in the dataset.

Earliest Recorded Watch Brand

The United Kingdom has the earliest recorded founding year in this dataset. Graham (1695) represents this early history, with 24 recorded watch brands from the country. This highlights the UK's historical precedence in the dataset.

Switzerland's Brand Concentration

Switzerland has the highest number of recorded brands, with 123 brands . Its earliest recorded brand, Blancpain (1735) , was founded later than Graham in the UK. This contrast illustrates that the earliest recorded founding year and the number of brands are different measures of watchmaking history.

Global Distribution of Active Watch Brands

Active watch brands are distributed worldwide, but their headquarters are not evenly spread across geographical regions. The clustering of green dots reveals areas with greater concentrations of brands, while other regions show fewer recorded headquarters. This highlights the uneven geographical distribution of the global watch industry.

Regional Concentration of Watch Brands

Europe, North America, and East Asia appear to contain noticeable concentrations of watch-brand headquarters in the map. These regional patterns suggest that active watch brands are geographically clustered rather than uniformly distributed worldwide. Exploring individual dots allows viewers to identify the countries and cities contributing to these concentrations.

3. Watch Characteristics

How have watch production, sizes, and materials varied over time?

Highest Recorded Count

Rolex records 33,710 watches in the 2010+ period, the highest count among the manufacturer-period combinations. The blue outline highlights this concentration of records in the dataset.

Increasing Median Watch Size

Typical watch sizes shift from around 35 mm in earlier periods to 40–41 mm after 1990. Before 1950, a second concentration near 50 mm suggests greater variation in recorded watch sizes.

How to Read the Chart

1. Identify the material using the chart legend and its corresponding colour.
2. Compare manufacturers by examining the size of each material segment.

Distinct Material Preferences

Steel dominates several manufacturers, while gold combinations and titanium contribute different shares across brands. Ceramic is also prominent in Hublot's material distribution.

4. Design and Value

How have watch prices changed over time and across manufacturer?

Rising Price Trend

Watch prices generally rise across production periods, with the highest median and widest upper price range in 2010+. This suggests that more recent watches in the dataset include higher-priced luxury models.

Luxury Price Gap

Patek Philippe has the highest average watch price, followed by Audemars Piguet. Their average prices are substantially higher than those of the other manufacturers, highlighting differences in luxury market positioning.

Steel: Rise to First

Steel rose from third to first place by 1970–1989 and maintained its lead afterwards.

Leather: Declining Rank

Leather fell from first to third place after 1969, indicating a shift in material ranking.

Gold: Stable Second Place

Gold returned to second place from 1970 onwards and remained stable in subsequent periods.
